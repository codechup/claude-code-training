## Objectives & prerequisites

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Say what `/dataviz` decides for you: chart form, colour by role, a validated palette, and mark, interaction and accessibility rules.
- Invoke it at the right moment — before the first line of chart code, not after.
- Replace its brand-neutral placeholder palette with your own without losing the guarantees.
- Recognise the two failures a chart review should always catch: the wrong form, and colour carrying meaning it cannot carry.

**Prerequisites.** Claude Code 2.1.198 or later. `/dataviz` is a bundled skill, so it is available unless bundled skills are turned off with `disableBundledSkills`. Lessons 01 and 02 of this module, if you plan to put the chart on an artifact — the CSP there decides which charting library, if any, the page may load.

<WhenNotToUse>
  `/dataviz` is design guidance for charts. It is not an analysis tool: it will not tell you whether
  your numbers are right, whether the sample is big enough, or whether the trend is real. Skip it
  when the honest answer is a number or a sentence — a single value on a slide does not need a
  chart, and a four-row table is a table. Skip it too when the destination renders charts itself
  from rows you hand it, because a picture of a chart loses hover, inspection and per-value
  comments. And do not reach for it to redecorate a chart whose form is wrong: no palette fixes a
  pie chart of a time series.
</WhenNotToUse>

## Concept

### What the skill does

`/dataviz [request]` is a bundled skill. Given data and a destination, Claude picks the chart form for the data, assigns colour by role, validates the palette for colourblind safety and contrast with a bundled script, and applies mark, interaction and accessibility rules. It works from a brand-neutral placeholder palette that you replace with your own.

Those four verbs are the whole value, and they map to the four ways charts usually go wrong: the wrong form, colour used decoratively, a palette nobody can read, and a mark that lies (a truncated axis, an area chart of something that does not accumulate).

This lesson's worked prompt names the data, the question and the destination — all three, because each changes the answer:

```text
/dataviz 30 days of tasks_created, tasks_completed and tasks_overdue from
artifacts/task-activity.csv, for a dashboard artifact. The question is whether
the backlog is growing.
```

The timing matters more than the wording: invoke it **before** the first line of chart code, before choosing colours, and before laying out a dashboard. Applied afterwards, it becomes a repaint of decisions already baked into the markup.

### Colour by role, not by decoration

Every recommendation there is a role. Three series on one scale — created, completed, overdue — get a **categorical** palette, assigned in a fixed order so a series does not change colour between charts. `avg_priority` sits on a different scale, so it does not get a second axis on the same chart; it gets its own small chart with a **single, sequential hue**. A legend is required once there is more than one series and pointless when there is only one. Text and axes stay neutral ink.

The interesting line is the one about `tasks_overdue`. The skill does not apply a rule mechanically: it points out that overdue is a "bad state" signal rather than a neutral category, and suggests the **status ramp** for it so a rising line reads as a problem instead of "series 3". That is a judgement call you have to make, not a default to accept — spending your status colours on a series means they no longer mean "something is wrong" anywhere else on the page. Decide once, per dashboard, and stay consistent.

That is the discipline: every colour on the chart has a job — identity, magnitude, or status — and no colour has two.

### Validating the palette

The part you cannot do by eye is the last one. Claude validates the palette with a bundled script rather than by judgement, checking colourblind safety and contrast; the week-27 release notes call it a runnable colour-palette validator. Two things follow.

First, **run it again after you swap in your brand palette.** The default is a brand-neutral placeholder, and a validated placebo palette says nothing about the four hues your brand hands you. The swap is the moment the guarantee is at risk.

Second, validation is a build step you can keep. A palette that passed once can fail after a designer nudges a hue, and the failure is invisible to everyone with normal colour vision — which is why it survives review.

### Charts on an artifact

If the chart is going on an artifact, lesson 01's constraints decide the implementation before any design question does. The page may load scripts from four CDN hosts only — `cdnjs.cloudflare.com`, `cdn.jsdelivr.net`, `cdn.tailwindcss.com` and `code.jquery.com` — every external image is blocked, and the rendered page must stay under 16 MiB. In practice that pushes you towards inline SVG for anything simple: no library to load, no blocked request, no size problem, and the text in the chart stays selectable and readable by a screen reader. Reach for a charting library only when the interaction genuinely needs one, and pin an exact version from one of the four hosts.

## Anti-patterns

**Calling `/dataviz` after the chart exists.** By then the form, the axes and the palette are already in the markup, and the skill can only suggest repainting. _Fix:_ invoke it before the first line of chart code.

**Two scales, one chart.** A dual axis lets you imply any correlation you like by choosing the ranges. _Fix:_ small multiples — a second, smaller chart sharing the x-axis.

**Colour doing two jobs.** Spending the status red on an ordinary series means red no longer signals a problem anywhere on the page. _Fix:_ categorical hues for identity, one ramp for magnitude, status colours reserved for status — and if a series really is a status signal, say so deliberately and keep every other chart on the page consistent with that choice.

**Swapping in the brand palette and shipping.** The validation you inherited was for the placeholder palette. _Fix:_ re-run the validator on the real colours, and keep it in the build.

**A picture of a chart where live rows were wanted.** Handing a rendered PNG to a destination that renders charts itself throws away hover, inspection and per-value comments. _Fix:_ hand over the rows.

**Trusting a legend to carry the message.** If the reader has to look up which line is which to understand the point, the point is not being made. _Fix:_ label the series you are arguing about directly, on the line.
