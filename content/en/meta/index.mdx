Meta is the part of the course about the course. [How this site was
built](/en/meta/how-this-site-was-built/) walks through this repository's own `CLAUDE.md`, rules,
hooks, skills, agents, plan files, content gates and deploy pipeline as a worked example of
everything taught in Level 4 — the same machinery you read about in
[m18-multi-session](/en/l4-master/m18-multi-session/) is what produced the page you are reading.
It is the case study; m18 is the lesson.

[Contributing](/en/meta/contributing/) covers the other direction: how to report a mistake,
propose a lesson or open a pull request, what your change is checked against before it merges, and
what the MIT licence means for anything you write here. [Sources
index](/en/meta/sources-index/) keeps one list of every source cited anywhere on the site, grouped
by the module that cites it, each with the date it was last verified.

There is no prerequisite for Meta; it is written for anyone curious about the site's own
construction, not only for people who have finished the curriculum. The repository is public and
MIT-licensed, so every file these pages mention can be read — and copied — at
[github.com/codechup/claude-code-training](https://github.com/codechup/claude-code-training).
